### <font color="red">GENERAL PSYCHIATRY & SUICIDALITY</font>
* In the <font color="red">peripartum period</font>, women are uniquely vulnerable to the acute onset and recurrence of psychiatric illness.
* <font color="red">Suicide risk</font> in the perinatal period is estimated at 1.6 to 4.5 per 100,000 live births in the United States.
* <font color="red">Maternal suicide rates</font> are likely underreported because studies often fail to account for deaths beyond the first 6 months postpartum.
* <font color="red">Postpartum blues</font> typically occur within the 1st week after parturition, peak on the 4th-5th day, and normalize by the 10th day.
* <font color="red">Psychotic symptoms</font> in the postpartum period are generally defined as symptoms occurring >6 months after delivery, whereas blues are early.
* <font color="red">EPDS (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale)</font> or <font color="red">PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire)</font> should be used to screen for depression at every contact during the first year postpartum.
* <font color="red">MDQ (Mood Disorder Questionnaire)</font> is the specific tool added to screen for bipolar disorder in pregnant and postpartum patients.
* <font color="red">Item 10 on the EPDS</font> and <font color="red">Item 9 on the PHQ-9</font> are the specific items that must be reviewed for positive answers regarding thoughts of self-harm.
* <font color="red">Suicidal ideation</font> management requires asking the patient directly about frequency and intent and requesting an emergent psychiatric evaluation.
* <font color="red">Significant others</font> should be contacted to assist in removing suicide methods (guns, pills) from the home for patients with suicidal ideation.
* <font color="red">Pharmacotherapy</font> for psychiatric disorders should strive for monotherapy to mitigate adverse effects on the fetus.
* <font color="red">Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)</font> is indicated for major mood disorders that are unresponsive to pharmacotherapy.
* <font color="red">Brexanolone</font> is a recent FDA-approved allopregnanolone analogue specifically for postpartum depression.
* <font color="red">Neonatal Adaptation Syndrome (NAS)</font> describes symptoms including respiratory, GI, and CNS issues in newborns exposed to SSRIs in utero.

### <font color="red">POSTPARTUM PSYCHOSIS & OCD</font>
* <font color="red">Postpartum psychosis</font> has an incidence of 1–2 per 1,000 births and is considered a psychiatric emergency.
* <font color="red">Altruistic delusions</font> in postpartum psychosis involve a mother killing her infant to save them from a "fate worse than death."
* <font color="red">Non-auditory hallucinations</font> (visual, olfactory, or tactile) are more common in postpartum psychosis compared to schizophrenia.
* <font color="red">Postpartum OCD</font> involves intrusive thoughts that are recognized by the patient as being misaligned with their moral values (ego-dystonic).
* <font color="red">Compulsions</font> in postpartum OCD must be time-consuming (>1 hour/day) and cause significant distress or impairment.
* <font color="red">Late-onset urea cycle disorder</font> is a rare differential for postpartum psychosis, presenting 1–3 days postpartum with confusion and rapid progression to coma.

### <font color="red">NEUROLOGICAL DISORDERS</font>
* <font color="red">Pregnancy-related GFR increase</font> leads to the excretion of B vitamins, requiring high-dose Vitamin B complex in the first trimester for peripheral neuropathies.
* <font color="red">Carpal tunnel syndrome</font> in pregnancy is caused by increased fluid retention and pressure on the median nerve in the wrist.
* <font color="red">Meralgia Paresthetica</font> presents as pain and numbness in the outer thigh during pregnancy.
* <font color="red">Multiple Sclerosis (MS)</font> is a T-cell–mediated autoimmune destruction of oligodendrocytes, often showing temporary improvement during pregnancy.
* <font color="red">MS in labor</font> may cause a lack of pelvic sensation, making it difficult for the mother to feel contractions or push effectively.
* <font color="red">Myasthenia Gravis (MG)</font> first-line treatment is the oral anticholinesterase <font color="red">pyridostigmine (Mestinon)</font>.
* <font color="red">Myasthenic crisis</font> involves severe muscle weakness and respiratory paralysis, often triggered by the stress of labor.
* <font color="red">Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)</font> is characterized by an acute onset of **symmetric ascending weakness** and loss of deep-tendon reflexes.
* <font color="red">Diagnosis of GBS</font> is primarily clinical, though lumbar puncture and EMG can provide supportive info. 
* <font color="red">Bell’s Palsy</font> shows an increased incidence in the <font color="red">3rd trimester</font>, potentially due to elevated cortisol-induced immunosuppression or perineural edema.
* <font color="red">Epilepsy preconceptional care</font> requires 0.4 mg/day of folic acid, increased to 4 mg/day for women already taking antiepileptic drugs.
* <font color="red">Valproate</font> is high-risk in pregnancy (4–8x risk), causing dose-dependent fetal malformations and lower cognitive performance.
* <font color="red">Lamotrigine and Levetiracetam</font> are considered lower-risk antiepileptic drugs for use during pregnancy.
* <font color="red">Status epilepticus</font> in pregnancy is a medical emergency treated with IV levetiracetam, fosphenytoin, or valproate.
* <font color="red">CT scanning</font> with abdominal shielding is generally safe and indicated for pregnant patients with sudden severe headaches to rule out hemorrhage.
* <font color="red">Prolactinoma</font> is the most common brain tumor that can cause endocrine disorders affecting lactation in postpartum patients.

### <font color="red">DERMATOLOGICAL DISORDERS</font>
* <font color="red">Melasma (Chloasma)</font>, the "mask of pregnancy," is caused by elevated estrogen, progesterone, and melanocyte-stimulating hormone.
* <font color="red">Linea nigra</font> and areolar hyperpigmentation often do not return to their original color after delivery.
* <font color="red">Striae Gravidarum</font> (stretch marks) affect up to 90% of pregnant women and appear perpendicular to the skin's tension lines.
* <font color="red">Jacquemier-Chadwick sign</font> refers to the erythema of the vestibule and vagina, while <font color="red">Goodell sign</font> is the bluish discoloration of the cervix.
* <font color="red">Acrochordons (Skin Tags)</font> develop during pregnancy due to hormonal shifts and increased skin friction (skin-on-skin contact).
* <font color="red">Atopic Eruption of Pregnancy (AEP)</font> is the **most common** pregnancy-specific dermatosis and is an umbrella term for eczema, prurigo, and pruritic folliculitis.
* <font color="red">Fluticasone propionate</font> should be **avoided** in pregnancy because the placenta does not metabolize it, allowing higher fetal concentrations.
* <font color="red">Polymorphic Eruption of Pregnancy (PEP)</font> is frequently seen in **primiparous women with multiple gestations** or large weight gain.
* <font color="red">Pemphigoid Gestationis (PG)</font> is a rare autoimmune disease where maternal **IgG** antibodies target the basement membrane, causing **tense bullae**.
* <font color="red">Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy (ICP)</font> requires delivery at **36 weeks** if bile acid levels are ≥100 micromoles/L.
* <font color="red">Pustular Psoriasis of Pregnancy (PPP)</font> can cause maternal **hypocalcemia**, leading to delirium, seizures, or tetany.
* <font color="red">Methotrexate and Mycophenolate Mofetil</font> are strictly **contraindicated** for treating dermatological conditions during pregnancy.

### <font color="red">COMPARISON AND DIFFERENTIATION FOR EXAMS</font>

1.  <font color="red">Postpartum Blues</font> peak at Day 4-5 and resolve by Day 10, whereas <font color="red">Postpartum Psychosis</font> is a psychiatric emergency presenting with delirium-like confusion.
2.  <font color="red">Postpartum OCD</font> involves ego-dystonic thoughts (moral alignment) and distress, while <font color="red">Postpartum Psychosis</font> features lack of insight and delusional/bizarre behavior.
3.  <font color="red">Tension Headaches</font> are the most common type and lack the nausea/vomiting associated with <font color="red">Migraine Headaches</font>.
4.  <font color="red">Migraines</font> typically improve during pregnancy, while <font color="red">Cluster Headaches</font> show no change in severity.
5.  <font color="red">Focal Onset Seizures</font> begin in one brain region and may or may not involve dyscognitive features, while <font color="red">Generalized Onset Seizures</font> (like Tonic-Clonic) affect both hemispheres and involve loss of consciousness.
6.  <font color="red">Valproate</font> has a 4-8x higher teratogenic risk compared to <font color="red">Lamotrigine</font>, which is a preferred lower-risk antiepileptic.
7.  <font color="red">MS</font> is characterized by T-cell autoimmune destruction, whereas <font color="red">Myasthenia Gravis (MG)</font> involves fluctuating weakness of ocular/respiratory muscles.
8.  <font color="red">Guillain-Barré (GBS)</font> results in **ascending** symmetric weakness, unlike <font color="red">Multiple Sclerosis</font>, which presents with focal/multifocal neurological episodes.
9.  <font color="red">PEP (PUPPP)</font> typically **spares the umbilicus** and begins in the abdominal striae, whereas <font color="red">Pemphigoid Gestationis (PG)</font> characteristically **involves the umbilicus**.
10. <font color="red">PG</font> is associated with fetal risks (preterm birth/placental insufficiency), but <font color="red">PEP</font> has **no adverse fetal outcomes**.
11. <font color="red">Atopic Eruption of Pregnancy (AEP)</font> is associated with Th2 immune shift, while <font color="red">Pemphigoid Gestationis (PG)</font> is an IgG-mediated autoimmune reaction.
12. <font color="red">Intrahepatic Cholestasis (ICP)</font> presents with pruritus of the **palms and soles** and elevated bile acids, whereas <font color="red">AEP</font> presents with primary eczematous skin lesions.
13. <font color="red">Pustular Psoriasis of Pregnancy (PPP)</font> is a dermatologic emergency involving **hypocalcemia** and sterile pustules, while <font color="red">PEP</font> is a common pruritic condition without systemic symptoms.
14. <font color="red">Sumatriptan</font> is used for severe migraines, but <font color="red">Ergotamines</font> must be strictly **avoided** in pregnancy.
15. <font color="red">URSODEOXYCHOLIC ACID (UDCA)</font> is the treatment of choice for maternal pruritus in <font color="red">ICP</font>, but it does not definitely improve fetal outcomes.

- The <font color="red">most common surgical procedure</font> performed during pregnancy is an <font color="red">appendectomy</font>. (Context: General Surgery in Pregnancy)
- <font color="red">Venous thromboembolism (VTE)</font> risk is significantly higher in pregnant women with malignancy compared to those without, particularly during the postpartum period. (Context: Surgery in Pregnancy)
- <font color="red">Sonography</font> is the preferred imaging modality during pregnancy and can be safely used in any trimester. (Context: Diagnostic Imaging)
- <font color="red">Gadolinium contrast</font> used in <font color="red">MRI</font> should not be used in the first trimester and used later only if benefits overwhelmingly outweigh risks. (Context: Diagnostic Imaging)
- <font color="red">Therapeutic radiation</font> directed to the maternal abdomen is strictly <font color="red">contraindicated</font> due to high doses. (Context: Radiation Therapy)
- During <font color="red">organogenesis</font> (weeks 2-8), the fetus is highly susceptible to radiation-induced <font color="red">fetal malformations</font> above threshold doses. (Context: Radiation Therapy)
- The <font color="red">fetal central nervous system</font> is most vulnerable to radiation-induced intellectual disability between <font color="red">8 and 25 weeks gestation</font>. (Context: Radiation Therapy)
- To prevent maternal and neonatal neutropenia or pancytopenia, <font color="red">chemotherapy</font> is typically withheld in the <font color="red">3 weeks before delivery</font>. (Context: Chemotherapy)
- <font color="red">Cytotoxic chemotherapy</font> is generally <font color="red">contraindicated during breastfeeding</font> due to drug transfer and limited neonatal clearance. (Context: Chemotherapy)
- <font color="red">Trastuzumab (Herceptin)</font>, while not teratogenic, is associated with <font color="red">oligohydramnios</font> when used in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters, though the effect is reversible. (Context: Target Therapy)
- <font color="red">Erythropoietin alfa</font>, used to stimulate hemopoiesis, appears safe in pregnancy but carries a potential risk of <font color="red">maternal hypertension</font>. (Context: Molecular Therapy)
- <font color="red">Ovarian transposition</font> is a surgical method to protect ovaries from pelvic radiation by fixing them to the lateral abdominal wall <font color="red">3 to 4 cm above the umbilicus</font>. (Context: Fertility Preservation)
- Prior <font color="red">abdominopelvic radiation</font> can adversely affect the uterus by reducing its volume, thinning the endometrium, and impairing blood flow, leading to increased rates of <font color="red">stillbirth and preterm birth</font>. (Context: Fertility Preservation)
- <font color="red">Malignant melanoma</font> is one of the most common types of <font color="red">placental metastasis</font>, though tumor cells are usually confined to the intervillous spaces. (Context: Placental Metastasis)

| DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS AND EXAM CONFUSERS |
| :--- |
- <font color="red">Luteoma of Pregnancy</font> vs. <font color="red">Hyperreactio Luteinalis</font>: Luteoma often presents as a **solid** mass and can cause **fetal virilization**, whereas HL presents with **multiple cysts ("spoke-wheel")** and **rarely** affects the fetus.
- <font color="red">Luteoma of Pregnancy</font> vs. <font color="red">OHSS</font>: Luteoma causes **androgen excess/virilization**, while OHSS presents with **third-spacing (ascites/pleural effusion)** and **no virilization**.
- <font color="red">OHSS</font> vs. <font color="red">Hyperreactio Luteinalis</font>: OHSS is usually **iatrogenic** (ovulation induction) and involves system-wide fluid shifts; HL is **spontaneous** (high hCG) and usually limited to ovarian enlargement.
- <font color="red">Cervical Microinvasion (IA1)</font> vs. <font color="red">Advanced Cervical CA</font>: IA1 (<3mm invasion) allows for **vaginal delivery** and deferred treatment; advanced stages (>IA1) usually require **Classical Cesarean** and immediate/neoadjuvant therapy.
- <font color="red">Radical Hysterectomy</font> vs. <font color="red">Radical Trachelectomy</font>: Hysterectomy removes the uterus; Trachelectomy removes the cervix but **preserves the uterine body** for future fertility.
- <font color="red">Breslow Scale</font> vs. <font color="red">Clark Classification</font> in Melanoma: Breslow measures **absolute thickness** (mm); Clark measures **anatomical level/depth** of invasion (epidermis/dermis).
- <font color="red">CA-125 in 1st Trimester</font> vs. <font color="red">3rd Trimester</font>: Elevation is **normal** in the 1st trimester; elevation in the 3rd trimester is **abnormal** and suggests preeclampsia or malignancy.
- <font color="red">Chemotherapy Timing</font>: Withheld **before 12-14 weeks** (teratogenesis) and **3 weeks before delivery** (neonatal cytopenia).
- <font color="red">Trastuzumab</font> vs. <font color="red">Standard Chemotherapy</font> in Breast CA: Trastuzumab is specifically linked to **oligohydramnios**; standard alkylating agents are linked to **IUGR/malformations** (if 1st tri).
- <font color="red">MRI Gadolinium</font> vs. <font color="red">CT Contrast</font>: Gadolinium is avoided in the **1st trimester**; CT contrast (iodinated) has **no known fetal harm** in pregnancy.
- <font color="red">Hodgkin</font> vs. <font color="red">Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma</font>: Hodgkin has an **excellent prognosis** (>90% survival) and Reed-Sternberg cells; Non-Hodgkin is more diverse and often more aggressive in pregnancy.
- <font color="red">Endocervical Polyp</font> vs. <font color="red">Cervical Cancer</font>: Both cause bleeding, but polyps are **benign fleshy masses** often causing AGUS, while cancer involves **ulceration/necrosis** and abnormal histology.
- <font color="red">Corpus Luteum Rupture</font> vs. <font color="red">Adnexal Torsion</font>: Rupture presents with **hemoperitoneum/collapsed cyst**; Torsion presents with an **enlarged, edematous mass** and the "whirlpool" sign.
- <font color="red">Myomectomy</font> vs. <font color="red">Uterine Artery Embolization</font>: Myomectomy is the **preferred** fertility-sparing surgical route; Embolization is **relatively contraindicated** for those desiring future pregnancy due to high complication rates.
- <font color="red">Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy</font> in Breast CA vs. Melanoma: Safe with **Technetium-99m** in pregnancy; blue dye (isosulfan/methylene) is generally avoided.
- <font color="red">Conglutinated Cervix</font> vs. <font color="red">Cervical Insufficiency</font>: Conglutination (stenosis) causes **failure to dilate**; Insufficiency causes **painless premature dilation**.
- <font color="red">Placental Metastasis: Melanoma</font> vs. <font color="red">others</font>: Melanoma is the **most common** to metastasize to the placenta; fetal involvement is rare but possible with melanoma.
- <font color="red">Thyroid CA Surgery Timing</font>: Ideally performed in the **2nd trimester** (after 14 weeks) to avoid miscarriage and anesthesia risks.
- <font color="red">Breastfeeding with Cancer</font>: Chemo is **contraindicated**; HPV vaccination is **compatible**; Radioiodine is **contraindicated**.
- <font color="red">Radiation Fetal Death</font> vs. <font color="red">Malformation</font>: Death occurs in the **first 2 weeks** (all-or-none); malformation happens during **organogenesis (weeks 2-8)**.
- <font color="red">Acute</font> vs. <font color="red">Chronic Leukemia</font>: Acute requires **emergency treatment** regardless of pregnancy; Chronic may allow for **deferred treatment** until after delivery.
- <font color="red">Vaginal Delivery in Cervical CA</font>: Safe for **Stage IA1**; **contraindicated** for bulky or advanced tumors due to hemorrhage risk.
- <font color="red">Adenomyosis</font> vs. <font color="red">Leiomyoma</font>: Adenomyosis is an **infiltrative** disruption of the junctional zone; Leiomyoma is a **well-circumscribed** benign tumor.
